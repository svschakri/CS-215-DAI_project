## Problem Statement

University students operate under a fixed 24-hour daily time budget, forcing continuous trade-offs
among academic study, sleep, physical exercise, extracurricular participation, and social
interaction. The primary analytical question addressed in this project is:

> **How do daily lifestyle choices and self-reported stress levels relate to a student's CGPA,
> and which daily habits actually make a difference in predicting academic performance when
> every student is limited to the same 24 hours a day?**

Every extra hour spent studying has to come from somewhere else — usually sleep, exercise, club
activities, or time with friends. This project untangles those daily time trade-offs to see which
routines genuinely drive CGPA and stress, and which apparent patterns are just side effects of a
fixed 24-hour day.

## Dataset

**Source:** [Student Lifestyle Dataset](https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/steve1215rogg/student-lifestyledataset) (Kaggle)

- **Size:** N = 2,000 student records × 8 columns
- **Quality:** 0 missing values, 0 duplicate rows
- **Special property:** the five daily activity columns always sum to exactly **24.0 hours** for
  every student (Study + Extracurricular + Sleep + Social + Physical = 24.0)

### What each column means

| Column | Type | Description |
|---|---|---|
| `Student_ID` | int | Unique student number, 1–2000 |
| `Study_Hours_Per_Day` | float (h/day) | Hours spent studying per day |
| `Extracurricular_Hours_Per_Day` | float (h/day) | Hours spent on clubs / extracurricular activities |
| `Sleep_Hours_Per_Day` | float (h/day) | Hours of sleep per day |
| `Social_Hours_Per_Day` | float (h/day) | Hours spent socialising with friends / others |
| `Physical_Activity_Hours_Per_Day` | float (h/day) | Hours of exercise / sports per day |
| `CGPA` | float (0–4.00 scale) | Cumulative Grade Point Average |
| `Stress_Level` | encoded int | Self-reported stress: **0 = Low, 1 = Medium, 2 = High** |

> Note: the CSV in this repo has been pre-cleaned by `data_clean.py` — stress labels were encoded
> to 0/1/2, numbers were formatted to 2 decimal places, and IDs were zero-padded (e.g. `0001`).

## Analysis Workflow (what the notebook contains)

1. **Loading the data** — shape, missing values, duplicates, summary statistics.
2. **Stress levels** — pie chart of students in each stress group.
3. **Daily routines** — histogram + KDE grids for the five activity columns and CGPA.
4. **CGPA and Stress** — box plots of CGPA by stress tier, and of each routine variable by stress tier.
5. **Group averages and correlation analysis** — mean CGPA / hours per stress tier and the Pearson correlation heatmap (lower triangle).
6. **Effect of the 24-hour constraint** — why the time-use data is *compositional* (only 4 degrees of freedom) and why one column must be dropped from regression.
7. **Linear regression** — CGPA regressed on Study, Extracurricular, Social and Physical hours via the normal equations, with R² computed step by step (Sleep omitted as the baseline).
8. **Additional analysis: Central Limit Theorem simulation** — 5,000 samples per size n ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10, 30, 100} drawn from Study hours, showing sample means converging to N(µ, σ²/n).

## Key Findings

1. **Stress distribution:** High stress is the largest group (1,029 students, 51.5%), followed by Medium (674, 33.7%) and Low (297, 14.8%).
2. **Study hours is the only habit that meaningfully predicts CGPA:** r = 0.73 with CGPA and r = 0.74 with Stress. Each extra study hour adds ≈ **+0.159 CGPA points**.
3. **Non-academic habits do not hurt grades:** Physical activity's raw negative correlation with CGPA (r = −0.34) disappears in the regression (β̂ ≈ +0.005) once Study hours is included — it was a mechanical side effect of the 24-hour day (Study vs Physical r = −0.49).
4. **Higher CGPA comes with higher stress:** the High-stress tier averages 3.26 CGPA with 8.39 h/day study and only 7.05 h/day sleep, while the Medium-stress routine (≈ 7.0 h study, ≈ 8.0 h sleep) sustains a healthy 3.02 CGPA.
5. **R² = 0.541:** the four routine variables explain 54.1% of CGPA variance (SST = 178.323, SSE = 81.856); the remaining 45.9% is due to unmeasured factors such as study quality, prior preparation, attendance, and course difficulty.
6. **CLT verified:** even though Study hours is flat and bounded (µ = 7.476, σ = 1.424), sample means rapidly form a normal bell curve, with empirical SD matching σ/√n (0.1437 vs 0.1424 at n = 100).
